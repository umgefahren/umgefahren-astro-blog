# Introduction

2023 was the first time I ever went to Korea. That was a lot of firsts!

- First country in Asia
- First country outside of Europe and America
- First flight over 10 hours
- First time in a country where I can't even read the letters (properly)

Places we visited:
- <HoverForHangul roman="Seoul" hangul="서울" />
- <HoverForHangul roman="Jeju" hangul="제주" />

# The flight

<ImageView image={Incheon} alt="Incheon" />

The flight to Korea is very long!

From `FRA` to `ICN` it is 13000 km. That is a long way! and travelling that long way takes about 11 hours.
But I have to say that my economy experience with Korean Air was my best economy experience so far.
The food was honestly the best airline food I ever had. The personal was very friendly and the movies were good too, 
but unfortunately they still don't support Bluetooth.

<FlightMap start={'FRA'} end={'ICN'} />

I don't know if anyone ever had a real culture shock from an airport, simply because all airports are seamingly the same.
So of course Incheon airport was no different.

<FunFact>
Did you know that Incheon airport is the international airport of Seoul (there is also Gimpo we will visit later) but it is not in Seoul?

It's actually in Incheon (oh wonder) which is a city about 50 km west of Seoul on an island.

If you don't believe me take a look at the map above.
</FunFact>

# The Arrival

As previously described Incheon is quite a bit outside of Seoul so the ride from the airport to the city took about 1 hour.
There was where the culture shock hit me. This city was so big that shortly after getting off the island you could already see big aparment complexes
and the city was already going to the horizon. 

<FunFact>
With a city population of 10 million people Seoul is the 9th largest city in the world.[^1]

If you count the metropolitan area it's the 4th largest city in the world.[^2] In fact nearly a third of the population of the whole korean peninsula lives in Seoul. [^3]

During our stay in Korea during early to mid september Korea was still quite hot and humid. Luckely our home had AC so we could escape the heat during the day and go out in the evening and what a city!

We where very fortunate that our home was in the nice neighbourhood of [<HoverForHangul roman="Jongno" hangul="종로구" />](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jongno), 
which is very near to the [<HoverForHangul roman="Gyeongbokgung" hangul="경복궁" />](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongbokgung) palace.

## [<HoverForHangul roman="Namsan" hangul="남산" />](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namsan)

<ImageView image={NamsanRiver} alt="Namsan River"/>

To everyone visiting Seoul, at least in the summer, I can recommend going to <HoverForHangul roman="Namsan" hangul="남산" /> park. It's a big park on a hill in the middle of the city,
in the middle it features a big tower called the [<HoverForHangul roman="N Seoul Tower" hangul="N서울타워" />](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N_Seoul_Tower) which is a great place to see the city from above
and iconic part of the Seoul skyline.

The park itself is a very beautiful place with many trees and long walk ways you can take to get to the top. From time to time there is also a cooling creek complementing the pretty atmosphere and light.
You almost forget that you are in the middle of one of the biggest cities in the world.[^1][^2]

At the top you can enjoy a beautiful view of Seoul, from where you can see where you have been and where you should go next. Very near to Namsan is one of the most famous parts of Seoul: [<HoverForHangul roman="Itaewon" hangul="이태원" />](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itaewon).

Also there is a cable car to get to the top.

## [<HoverForHangul roman="Jogyesa" hangul="조계사" />](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jogyesa)

<ImageView image={JogyesaSide} alt="Jogyesa temple from the side"/>

[<HoverForHangul roman="Jogyesa" hangul="조계사" />](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jogyesa) is an imporant temple in Korean Buddhism. It's history dates back to 1395, the dawn of the [Joseon Dynasty].
It has an important role in the time of japanase occupation, where it became one of the strongest fortresses of Korean Buddhism.

To see the temple at night, while still illuminated and with people praying was a very special experience. It was a sight of intense beauty, peace and tranquility.
The mood in the air was very difficult to capture with my camera, but I hope the picture above gives you a glimpse of what I felt.

## [<HoverForHangul roman="Deoksugung" hangul="덕수궁" />](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deoksugung)

I also want to mention the [<HoverForHangul roman="Deoksugung" hangul="덕수궁" />](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deoksugung) palace.
It's significance stems from the inhabitance of the last king of Korea, before the country was annexed by Japan in 1910.
Although it's smaller then [<HoverForHangul roman="Gyeongbokgung" hangul="경복궁" />](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongbokgung), you can better imagine people living there then <HoverForHangul roman="Gyeongbokgung" hangul="경복궁" />.

<FunFact>
There is an urban legend saying that all couples who walk down the Stonewall walkway are fated to break-up. So if you are in a relationship and you want to stay in it, you should avoid walking down the Stonewall walkway 😉.[^5]
</FunFact>

## [<HoverForHangul roman="Bukchon Hanok Village" hangul="북촌한옥마을" />](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukchon_Hanok_Village) and [<HoverForHangul roman="Seoul Museum of Art" hangul="서울시립미술관" />](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Museum_of_Art)

I think about both places there is enough written on the internet so I will keep it short.

[<HoverForHangul roman="Bukchon Hanok Village" hangul="북촌한옥마을" />](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukchon_Hanok_Village) is a very beautiful place with many traditional houses and a great view of the city. It's a great place to take pictures and enjoy the view. Very touristy!

The [<HoverForHangul roman="Seoul Museum of Art" hangul="서울시립미술관" />](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Museum_of_Art) is a very modern museum with many interesting exhibitions. It's a great place to spend a rainy day, great cafetria. But the exhibition is not very big.
